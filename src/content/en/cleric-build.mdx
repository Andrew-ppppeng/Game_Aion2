A useful Cleric build starts with two jobs: keep the party able to act, and contribute damage when it is safe. Prepare recovery tools first, then build an attack sequence around the debuffs and skills your character actually has. The examples here compare [BoredAF's TW-experience guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J3nPw7cuDjY) with [aLuckyRO's low-level Global simulation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UPjsUVC0DtA); neither is an independently tested final Global allocation.

<h2 id="choose-your-cleric-setup">Choose the setup for today's content</h2>

| Situation | Starting emphasis | Adjustment trigger |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solo questing | Reliable damage with self-recovery available | Add recovery when pulls interrupt your progress |
| A regular dungeon party | Healing, cleanse, resurrection, and useful boss debuffs | Add an emergency heal if routine damage becomes difficult |
| Party with a Chanter | Check the Chanter's buff coverage before duplicating protection | Swap only after reading the actual effects and agreeing coverage |
| Party without a Chanter | Consider the party protection option demonstrated in the guides | Preserve it if its protection or damage benefit is needed |
| Learning a high-pressure encounter | Give healing and defensive reactions more room | Trade a marginal damage option for the tool that addresses the wipe |

BoredAF describes maintaining debuffs while recovering from mistakes; aLuckyRO changes the setup according to Chanter presence. Do not read this as permission to ignore injured allies. A missed lethal mechanic may require resurrection, while ordinary damage or a removable debuff calls for a different response.

<h2 id="starting-stigma-example">A concrete starting Stigma example</h2>

BoredAF proposes a four-option PvE setup for his expected early Global environment. Treat the number of slots as his model and use only the slots visible in your client.

| Option in the guide | Why it is considered | When to reconsider it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Party damage/protection buff | Adds useful party offense and protection | A Chanter may already cover overlapping effects |
| Resurrection | Restores an ally after a death | Keep it accessible when the group relies on you for recovery |
| Earth's Punishment | Supports the demonstrated damage and debuff sequence | Check the available skill level and interaction in your tooltip |
| Noble Aura | Adds continuing damage | Replace with an extra healing option if the encounter needs it |

He also discusses Prayer of Amplification as a possible alternative, and Absolution or Benevolence when more healing is needed. Those are choices to test, not proven identical damage substitutes. The two guides' captions disagree on the English protection skill name, so the table uses its function rather than declaring an automatic transcript to be the official name. See the [original Stigma explanation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J3nPw7cuDjY).

aLuckyRO recommends unlocking resurrection early instead of postponing it until it is heavily upgraded. Start with the available version, then assess whether additional investment improves the cast sufficiently for your party. His low-level example is especially useful when your point budget cannot reproduce a developed TW character.

<h3>Two choices to make before the dungeon</h3>

**A Chanter covers the party buff, but routine damage overwhelms your healing.** First compare the actual effects together. If the protection benefit is already covered, evaluate the slot you can change; BoredAF's alternatives include Absolution or Benevolence when more healing is needed. You can also reconsider his damage-oriented fourth choice, Noble Aura. Test whether the change solves recovery before removing other damage tools. Similar-looking buff names alone do not prove that coverage is equivalent.

**There is no Chanter, and the copied Condemnation reset does not happen.** Keep the party protection under consideration and preserve resurrection access. Test Debilitating Mark, Chains of Torment, and Earth's Punishment manually with your current options. Use Condemnation when it is genuinely available instead of pretending you have the developed guide's reset loop. Spending more points elsewhere will not establish an interaction your selected options do not support.

<h2 id="damage-and-debuff-order">Damage and debuff order</h2>

BoredAF's demonstrated order is Debilitating Mark, Chains of Torment, and Earth's Punishment before repeatedly using Condemnation as its opportunities become available. These names describe his guide; match them to your client. He explains a critical-hit interaction that changes how often Condemnation can be used. Do not assume that interaction is active until your available skill options support it.

Begin manually: apply the intended debuffs, use the available damage skills, and watch for the expected reset. If the reset does not occur, inspect skill levels, selected options, and the target rather than copying more buttons into a sequence. A new character's cooldowns differ from an established character's. [aLuckyRO's early setup](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UPjsUVC0DtA) makes that limitation central to his approach.

<h2 id="healing-cleanse-and-resurrection">Healing, cleanse, and resurrection decisions</h2>

Keep a direct heal, a cleanse-capable option, and resurrection on reachable controls outside a repeating damage sequence. Look at the party and encounter before committing a major recovery button. Removing the cause of damage can be more useful than repeatedly healing through it.

BoredAF demonstrates a cleanse option on Radiant Recovery and recommends pursuing a stronger Healing Light configuration as progression permits. Those are later skill-development goals, not requirements you should pretend to meet on a fresh character. Read the current specialization text and select the option explicitly. When an ally dies, choose a safe casting window; attempting a resurrection in a lethal mechanic can leave the group with two dead players. His [recovery discussion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J3nPw7cuDjY) keeps mechanics and healing responsibilities together.

<h3>Read the failure before choosing a recovery button</h3>

| What happened | Useful response to check | What to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| An ally takes ordinary damage | An available direct heal and a safe cast position | Continuing damage while recovery is urgently needed |
| A harmful removable effect persists | Whether your selected skill includes the required cleanse | Assuming Radiant Recovery cleanses without the demonstrated option |
| An ally dies during a dangerous mechanic | Resurrection availability and a safe casting window | Starting a cast that makes you the next casualty |
| The party is stable but debuffs have expired | Reapply the intended debuffs, then resume available damage | Assuming the later reset exists because the skill name matches |

**Must I reach the guide's later Healing Light setup before grouping?** It is a progression goal in BoredAF's example, not a verified entry requirement. Assess the current encounter and your available recovery.

**Should I cast resurrection immediately?** Restore the ally when the casting window is safe. Another death caused by ignoring the mechanic can make recovery harder for the whole party.

<h2 id="gear-and-daevanion">Gear and Daevanion priorities without fake thresholds</h2>

BoredAF considers attack, critical effects, double-attack effects, sufficient accuracy, and cooldown reduction in his developed-character example. Use these as attributes to compare, not as universal numeric targets. The right tradeoff depends on your current equipment, content, and whether you are failing to hit, survive, or heal effectively.

Before spending scarce resources, compare a proposed Daevanion route with the skills and passives you use. Avoid investing in a later build's path solely to match its screenshot. Save your current region, level, selected skill options, and equipped Stigmas alongside the plan. The [builds guide](/builds) provides a repeatable setup checklist.

<h2 id="older-guides-and-pvp">Older guides and PvP builds</h2>

Some early regional Cleric notes deprioritize skills that newer videos use for damage. NC has published [regional class and Stigma changes](https://about.ncsoft.com/en/news/article/aion2_update_260325), so date and progression differences matter. Keep the source context rather than combining incompatible recommendations into a single supposed best build.

This page gives a PvE starting framework. Before entering [PvP](/pvp), reconsider survival, target pressure, healing access, and your team's role. A TW endgame equipment list or a PvE damage sequence alone does not establish a Global battle-healer build.
