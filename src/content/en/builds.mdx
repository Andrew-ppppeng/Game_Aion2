An AION 2 build is more than a class name and a list of equipped skills. Match it to your region, progression, activity, and party job. Start with a setup you can operate using the options you have unlocked, then adjust it when an encounter exposes a specific problem.

<h2 id="check-region-and-progression">Check region and progression before copying</h2>

Keep these details beside any setup you save:

- Region and client or patch date.
- Character level and intended activity: solo, party PvE, or PvP.
- Actual skill levels and selected specialization options.
- Unlocked Stigma slots, equipped skills, and available upgrade points.
- Relevant Daevanion choices, equipment, and party support.

An unlocked Stigma skill is not necessarily equipped, and a guide can show more slots than your character currently has. In his [Assassin tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DNfd1lZtQkI), aLuckyRO separates an expected early Global four-slot model from later KR/TW six-slot characters. Confirm the actual limits in your client instead of treating either number as universal.

The regional game has also received [class and Stigma changes](https://about.ncsoft.com/en/news/article/aion2_update_260325) and [Chapter 1 progression changes](https://about.ncsoft.com/en/news/article/aion2_update_260706). These announcements explain why later regional screenshots can differ; they do not establish every Global launch limit.

<h2 id="skills-stigmas-and-daevanion">Build skills, Stigmas, and Daevanion together</h2>

First, identify the skills that perform your main job. Raise useful skills within your point budget, then explicitly select their unlocked options. aLuckyRO's [Chanter demonstration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EsxGbxt0k-4) shows why spending points alone can leave a new specialization unselected.

Next, choose Stigma skills for the current encounter rather than selecting every damage option by default. A Cleric party setup needs recovery access; a Chanter's support choices depend on whether another healer is present. Finally, inspect Daevanion routes for the skills or passives your setup actually uses. A developed character's board is not automatically an efficient route for a new one.

Change one part at a time and test it. If a suggested effect does not occur, check the current tooltip, selected option, and prerequisite before changing equipment around an assumption.

<h2 id="three-concrete-examples">Three concrete starting examples</h2>

For **Assassin**, aLuckyRO demonstrates activating buffs, reaching the target, using the attack combination, building the required resource, and spending it on a finisher. The lesson is to understand those relationships manually before copying a later-character sequence. The tutorial's automatic captions are unreliable for several skill names, so they are not converted here into a purported official button-by-button macro.

For **Chanter**, his low-level example prioritizes Undefeated Mantra and a workable Dark Crush loop. Add healing or protection when the group's composition and encounter require it. A later skill option changes the loop, so the same button order may not fit an earlier character.

For **Cleric**, the [BoredAF example](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J3nPw7cuDjY) combines party protection, resurrection, Earth's Punishment, and a damage or healing fourth choice in its expected early model. Keep the recovery tools available and reconsider overlapping buffs when a Chanter is present. Follow the class pages for the source limits and the choices behind each example.

These are functional starter examples from the guides above, not guaranteed final loadouts. Write your actual chosen skills and options beside the relevant row. If your slots or points are insufficient, preserve the job the party relies on you to perform before adding a marginal damage choice.

<h2 id="combos-and-reactive-skills">Separate repeatable attacks from reactive decisions</h2>

Learn the loop manually first. Then use the game's own combo controls for attacks you genuinely want repeated in that order. Keep emergency healing, resurrection, defensive reactions, and situational buffs accessible separately. A high-level character's attack speed or cooldown reduction can make its sequence unsuitable for yours.

[Madsin's systems overview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Tn7WKMXt5Wo) explains built-in sequences and the need to keep contextual skills outside them. This page's setup process concerns those in-game controls. Test against a moving enemy and an encounter mechanic, not only a stationary target.

<h3>A useful retest: one change, one question</h3>

If a Chanter follow-up fails to appear, first test its trigger with the selected option. If a Cleric reset fails, verify the interaction before adding another damage button. If an Assassin finisher feels random, stop the automatic sequence and watch the resource rise and fall manually. After an isolated test works, repeat it while moving; then check whether you can interrupt to perform your party job.

Record a result such as "the opener produces the follow-up with this option" or "this sequence leaves recovery inaccessible." This is more actionable than saving only a damage total.

**Why did more skill points not change the combo?** In the demonstrated interface, unlocking an option does not select it automatically. Verify the selected specialization and its conditions.

**Where are exact point tables for every class?** The verified material here does not support current Global allocations for all eight classes. Use the documented starters and the setup checks rather than treating a regional screenshot as a universal answer.

<h2 id="save-and-improve-your-build">Save a plan and improve the weakest part</h2>

You can inspect an external [Character Builder](https://codexgames.net/aion-2/character-builder/guest) or [Aion2Hub's Cleric directory](https://aion2hub.com/builds/cleric/) for planning examples. These are third-party tools. The directory distinguishes Global test examples from KR/TW data; its score is not this wiki's measured damage result.

After a run, identify the actual failure. Missing hits calls for checking accuracy and the target; deaths call for mechanics and survival; failed skill interactions call for options and timing; party instability calls for recovery coverage. Adjust that problem first. Keep a working configuration saved before experimenting, and review [PvP modes](/pvp) separately when changing from PvE to competitive play.
