An AION 2 build is more than a class name and a list of equipped skills. Match it to your region, progression, activity, and party job. Start with a setup you can operate using the options you have unlocked, then adjust it when an encounter exposes a specific problem.

Build your setup in four parts: skills, selected specializations, equipped Stigmas, and a manual combat loop.

<h2 id="check-region-and-progression">Match the setup to your character</h2>

Keep these details beside any setup you save:

- Region, character level, and activity: solo, party PvE, or PvP.
- Skill levels and selected specialization options.
- Unlocked Stigma slots, equipped skills, and available upgrade points.
- Daevanion choices, equipment, and party support.

Unlocking a Stigma and equipping it are separate steps. Fill your available slots with the skills your role needs. More developed characters may have additional slots and options, so build the core loop before adding extras.

<h2 id="skills-stigmas-and-daevanion">Build skills, Stigmas, and Daevanion together</h2>

Identify the skills that perform your main job. Upgrade useful skills within your point budget, then select their unlocked specialization options manually. Spending points alone does not activate a newly unlocked option.

Next, choose Stigma skills for the current encounter rather than selecting every damage option by default. A Cleric party setup needs recovery access; a Chanter's support choices depend on whether another healer is present. Finally, inspect Daevanion routes for the skills or passives your setup actually uses. A developed character's board is not automatically an efficient route for a new one.

Change one part at a time and test it. If a suggested effect does not occur, check the current tooltip, selected option, and prerequisite before changing equipment around an assumption.

<h2 id="three-concrete-examples">Three concrete starting examples</h2>

For **Assassin**, activate useful buffs, reach the target, build the required resource with attacks, and spend it on a finisher. Learn resource generation and consumption manually before combining the buttons.

For **Chanter**, prioritize Undefeated Mantra and a working Dark Crush loop. Add healing or protection when the party needs it, and adjust the timing to your selected cooldown option.

For **Cleric**, combine party protection, resurrection, Earth’s Punishment, and a damage or healing choice. Keep recovery accessible and coordinate overlapping buffs with a Chanter.

Practice the listed loops manually before saving them. If slots or points are insufficient, preserve the job the party relies on you to perform before adding damage.

<h2 id="combos-and-reactive-skills">Separate repeatable attacks from reactive decisions</h2>

Learn the loop manually first. Then use the game's own combo controls for attacks you genuinely want repeated in that order. Keep emergency healing, resurrection, defensive reactions, and situational buffs accessible separately. A high-level character's attack speed or cooldown reduction can make its sequence unsuitable for yours.

Test the sequence against a moving enemy and an encounter mechanic. Make sure you can stop attacking to move, defend, or recover an ally.

<h3>A useful retest: one change, one question</h3>

If a Chanter follow-up fails to appear, first test its trigger with the selected option. If a Cleric reset fails, verify the interaction before adding another damage button. If an Assassin finisher feels random, stop the automatic sequence and watch the resource rise and fall manually. After an isolated test works, repeat it while moving; then check whether you can interrupt to perform your party job.

Record a result such as "the opener produces the follow-up with this option" or "this sequence leaves recovery inaccessible." This is more actionable than saving only a damage total.

**Why did more skill points not change the combo?** Unlocking an option does not select it automatically. Select the specialization and check its trigger condition.

**What should I upgrade first with limited points?** Secure your main attacks and required support, select the options that keep them working, then improve their passives and Daevanion upgrades.

<h2 id="talent-calculator">Talent calculator: plan skills with a Global tool</h2>

For an **AION 2 talent calculator**, use a skill planner that matches your region and character progression. The [Global Build Planner](https://aion2.gaming.tools/build-planner) combines gear, skills and Daevanion; its **Skills** tab is the starting point for a skill-point plan.

1. Select your class and faction.
2. Open **Skills** and inspect the displayed skill-point and Stigma budgets.
3. Use **Raise** beside a skill to add a level; **Lower** reverses it. Watch the used-points counter before investing in another skill.
4. Review Active, Passive and Stigma choices separately. Preserve the recovery or defensive skill your role needs.
5. Apply only the levels and options your character can actually use in game.

The plan stays in the current browser and survives a page reload. **Save to My Builds** requires an account for the site's saved-build workflow. Keep a separate copy of your choices before clearing browser data or changing devices. The planner's totals are planning aids; use your character's current tooltips and unlocked slots when applying the plan.

Do not copy a KR/TW allocation into Global just because the class name matches.

<h2 id="save-and-improve-your-build">Save a plan and improve the weakest part</h2>

[Aion2Hub’s Cleric directory](https://aion2hub.com/builds/cleric/) separates Global Launch Scale Test examples from KR/TW builds. KR/TW equipment, stats and Gear Scores are not valid for Global; a test-client example may also differ from launch. Choose an example for your service and compare it with your available skills before applying it.

After a run, identify the actual failure. Missing hits calls for checking accuracy and the target; deaths call for mechanics and survival; failed skill interactions call for options and timing; party instability calls for recovery coverage. Adjust that problem first. Keep a working configuration saved before experimenting, and review [PvP modes](/pvp) separately when changing from PvE to competitive play.

Use the [Gladiator](/gladiator), [Ranger](/ranger), and [Spiritmaster](/spiritmaster) pages for starting skill choices. The [macro guide](/macro-guide) explains in-game skill combinations; [Notmeter](/notmeter) covers the separate tool.
