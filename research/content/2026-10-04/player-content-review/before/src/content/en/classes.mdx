Global currently presents eight classes: Gladiator, Templar, Assassin, Ranger, Chanter, Cleric, Sorcerer, and Spiritmaster. Start with the combat job you enjoy, then compare how that job changes between solo play, dungeons, and PvP. The roster below follows [NC's Global class introduction](https://aion2.plaync.com/en-us/about/index).

<h2 id="choose-a-role">Choose by the responsibility you want</h2>

For a frontline character, compare Templar with Gladiator. Templar puts the party's safety and enemy positioning at the center of your decisions. Gladiator suits players who want a more offensive melee identity with defensive options. A tank still needs to learn mechanics: durability does not make a dangerous attack safe for everyone behind you.

For ranged damage, compare Ranger, Sorcerer, and Spiritmaster by how you want to deliver that damage. Ranger favors physical ranged attacks; Sorcerer revolves around spell timing; Spiritmaster adds elemental spirit management. Watching a long boss encounter is more useful than choosing from one spectacular burst clip.

Assassin is a useful shortlist choice if you enjoy close-range execution, positioning, and committing during attack windows. Try its movement and target access before investing heavily: an unfamiliar boss or connection delay can make a rehearsed sequence feel very different. These are selection tips based on the [gameplay examples](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RXYBucBwRtk), rather than guaranteed class performance.

<h3>A two-class tryout with a useful result</h3>

Start with a question you can observe. For Templar versus Gladiator, ask whether you enjoy taking responsibility for where the enemy stands or prefer spending more attention on your own attack opportunities. For Ranger versus Sorcerer, watch whether maintaining distance or finding a safe cast window feels more natural. For Spiritmaster, include managing spirits in the test; a single large damage number does not show that extra work.

Use the same kind of enemy and comparable progression. Note three things after each try: which attacks you could keep using while moving, which mechanic made you stop, and whether you could reach the required defensive or recovery control. This is a practical comfort test, not a fair damage benchmark between unfinished characters.

<h2 id="cleric-or-chanter">Cleric or Chanter?</h2>

Choose Cleric if you want the most direct responsibility for healing, cleansing, and recovery. Choose Chanter if you prefer contributing melee damage while improving the party through buffs and situational support. Their responsibilities overlap, but a Chanter build intended for damage should not automatically be treated as a replacement for a dedicated healer.

Grobs describes Chanter support as comfortable in suitable groups, with limitations becoming more visible under sustained pressure. His explanation also notes that some Cleric and Chanter buffs can overlap. Before a dungeon, agree who handles recovery and check the actual buff descriptions instead of assuming that every similar effect stacks. Follow the [Chanter guide](/chanter) or [Cleric build guide](/cleric-build) for starting configurations.

<h2 id="solo-party-and-pvp">Your main activity changes the decision</h2>

Solo play rewards a character you can operate consistently while handling mechanics yourself. Party play also rewards protection, debuffs, recovery, and reliable positioning. PvP adds target selection and reactions against opponents who do not follow a fixed boss script.

Write down your first priority: relaxed questing, organized dungeons, competitive PvP, or a mixture. Then shortlist two classes and inspect their gameplay in that activity. Use the [tier-list comparison](/tier-list) as a record of named players' opinions, and the [builds guide](/builds) to check whether a suggested setup fits your level and available skills.

<h3>Before your first party: assign the work</h3>

A Templar candidate should agree who starts the pull and where the group wants the enemy positioned. A Cleric candidate should make party targeting, a heal, a cleanse-capable skill if unlocked, and resurrection easy to reach. A Chanter candidate should confirm whether the party expects emergency support or its main recovery. Damage dealers should practice returning to a safe attack position after a mechanic rather than continuing a rehearsed sequence into danger.

**Do I need to pick the highest-ranked class to play with friends?** First check the responsibility the group lacks and whether you enjoy doing it. A needed role you can operate consistently is a more useful starting decision than a letter from a mixed-content ranking.

**What if I like a class but dislike its party job?** Inspect a longer encounter in your intended activity before investing further. Solo comfort alone will not tell you whether you enjoy tank positioning, recovery decisions, or buff management.

<h2 id="brawler-and-regional-guides">Why do some guides list nine classes?</h2>

NC introduced Brawler in the earlier operating version through [Chapter 1: Lands of Sand and Snow on July 6, 2026](https://about.ncsoft.com/en/news/article/aion2_update_260706). That update also expanded progression. It does not establish that Brawler is a selectable ninth Global class on the roster checked here.

When a guide shows Brawler, later equipment, or different skill limits, inspect its region and update date. Use it for context only after confirming that the same feature exists in your client. For a first character, choose from the eight classes displayed by the Global site and coordinate your faction and server with friends using the [server guide](/server).

For practical starting choices, compare the [Gladiator guide](/gladiator), [Ranger leveling guide](/ranger) and [Spiritmaster guide](/spiritmaster). Each separates its regional build examples from verified Global class information.
