Overview
Looking for the best AION 2 settings? This guide shows you which combat, graphics, camera, HUD, and targeting options are worth changing, how to get more FPS, and what the most important keyboard shortcuts do.

all the most important AION 2 keyboard shortcuts and keybinds at a glance
AION 2
’s Settings menu is huge. There are eight main tabs with additional sub-tabs, and several options can noticeably change combat, visibility, and performance. I went through the entire menu during the Launch Scale Test and tested the settings that matter most. Below you’ll find my recommended starting points, followed by every important option in detail.
The eight tabs are Combat, Key Settings, Info Display, Graphics, Accessibility, Audio, Notification, and Miscellaneous. Every page has a Reset button at the bottom that only restores the current page to its default values, so you can experiment without much risk. You can find all our other guides in our

Best AION 2 Combat Settings: Control Mode and Targeting
Under Combat > Controls, the first option is
[PC] Control Mode
. During the test, it was set to AION 2, which uses a reticle and lets you adjust its vertical position, set to 120 pixels by default. You can switch between control modes at any time with the minus key. Try both versions during your first evening before getting too used to one.
Below that, you decide how the game handles targets. Target Tracking and Auto Target upon Skill Use were disabled during the test, while Set Main Target upon Attack was enabled. If you prefer aiming manually, leave it that way. If you want to miss fewer spells while questing, enable automatic target selection.

Under Combat > Controls, you select the Control Mode and decide how the game acquires targets. © S4G / NCSOFT
Under Target Search, you determine the order in which the Tab key selects targets. Ten priority levels can be freely arranged, ranging from targets attacking you and hostile players to regular monsters. The default order works well for leveling, but it’s worth taking another look at this setting for PvP in the Abyss.
Best Auto Use Settings: Potions and Buffs
Under Combat > Auto Use, you decide when the game assists you. AION 2 automatically uses Life Potions by default when you drop below 70 percent HP. That’s fairly early. Lower the threshold if you want to conserve potions, or leave it as is if you prefer playing it safe. You can also access automatic potion use while playing with the X key.
Below that are the Buff Auto-Use Conditions. During the test, they were set to
In Combat or Motion
. This lets your character automatically refresh buffs without you having to think about them.

Best Loot Settings in AION 2: Filter Item Pickup
Under Combat > Looting, you decide what your pickup key collects. For equipment, you can filter by rarity, from Common through Rare, Epic, Unique, and Legendary. For other loot types, you can choose whether to pick up potions, common consumables, Seal Stones, and crafting materials.
Everything is enabled by default. Your Cube, AION 2’s inventory, had 60 slots during the test, and those fill up quickly while leveling. Common equipment still provides materials when dismantled, but it also clogs up your Cube. If you’re only going to dismantle that gear anyway, the filter can save you a lot of clicks.
The item pickup options let you decide which equipment rarities and loot types you collect. © S4G / NCSOFT
Best AION 2 Graphics Settings for More FPS
Under Graphics > Optimize, you can adjust Display Mode, Resolution, Vertical Sync, and Refresh Rate. Ultrawide works: During the test, I played at 3440 x 1440 pixels and 165 Hz in Borderless Window mode. Below that are the graphics presets and individual quality settings, ranging from Texture Quality to Anti-Aliasing.
Ad
Ad
These are the settings I would check first if you want to improve FPS in AION 2, especially in crowded cities, dungeons, and large-scale battles:
Upscaler:
DLSS or FSR, along with Resolution Scale. During the test, I used Quality
Frame Generator:
can only be enabled when an upscaler is active. With DLSS, you need an NVIDIA RTX 40-series GPU or newer. On Windows 11, it works immediately; on Windows 10, you need to enable the feature manually and restart your PC
Mass Combat Quality:
controls how many characters are displayed at the same time. In crowded cities and large battles, this is the first setting I would lower
Player Particle Effect Display:
was set to you and your party members. Effects from other players are hidden, which helps performance in large groups
Same Appearance for Other Players:
displays other characters with a standardized appearance. You lose some visual variety, but it should improve performance in crowded areas
NVIDIA Reflex:
was set to Boost during the test and helps reduce input latency
The Steam page recommends the Very Low preset for Full HD on the minimum hardware and the Low preset on the recommended hardware. If your PC is close to the minimum requirements, start low and raise the settings one by one.
Frame Generator can only be enabled when an Upscaler is active. With DLSS, you need an RTX 40-series GPU or newer. © S4G / NCSOFT
Best Camera Settings: Distance, Shake, and Teleport Effects
Under Graphics > Camera / Direction, you can adjust the maximum camera distance, which was set to Far during the test, along with combat camera shake and separate horizontal and vertical camera movement speeds. If the amount of visual movement makes you uncomfortable, turn off camera shake.
Ad
Ad
The same page contains two options I recommend changing:
Teleport Effect Color:
defaults to Light. If the bright flashes during teleportation bother you, switch it to Dark
Show World Map Transition Effects:
I had this turned off, so the map opens without a transition animation
You can also decide whether Title Effects appear only on your own nameplate and whether your weapon remains visible outside combat.
Under Camera / Direction, you can adjust camera distance and movement speed as well as the Teleport Effect Color. © S4G / NCSOFT
Best HUD Settings in AION 2: Customize the Interface
HUD Edit lets you rearrange the interface to your liking. Party frames, the boss bar, minimap, quest list, chat, and quickslots can all be moved and resized. A grid and snapping function help you line everything up cleanly.
Ad
Ad
One particularly useful feature: There are six preset slots, and your layouts can be exported and imported. For example, you can create one layout for questing and another for group content.
HUD Edit is also where you’ll find UI Scale and Chat Font Size, both set to Medium by default. If the chat text is too small, increase it by one step here.
HUD Edit lets you reposition every interface element and save up to six layouts. © S4G / NCSOFT
Combat Visibility: Boss Orientation, Skill Range, and Notifications
Under Info Display > Combat, you can display the range of your skills and enemies’ aggro range, both with adjustable transparency.
Display Boss Orientation
is disabled by default and offers several levels. Try it during your first few dungeons.
Ad
Under Notification, the game warns you when you’re hit or when your HP is low, with the default low-HP threshold set to 20 percent.
AION 2 Language, Voice, and Accessibility Settings
During the test, the interface was available in German, Korean, English, Japanese, and French. There is no German voice-over; you can choose between English, Korean, and Japanese. Both settings are found under Miscellaneous > Language. Steam lists eight supported languages for release, adding Spanish, Brazilian Portuguese, and Russian.
Under Accessibility, you’ll find color-blindness correction with adjustable intensity, Brightness Reduction for light-sensitive players, and scrolling-text speed. There are also subtitles for cutscenes, while the Communication section lets you adjust the font size and color of each chat channel.

Expedition, Daily Dungeon, Nightmare, Transcendence, Shugo Festival, Abyss, Arena, and the World Market are among the menus without a default hotkey. If you open these windows frequently, assign them a key yourself under Key Settings > Menu.
Mouse Sensitivity and FPS Limit: What Was Missing in the Test
In the Launch Scale Test, there were two settings I couldn’t find: a dedicated mouse-sensitivity slider and a traditional frame-rate limiter. The mouse felt very sensitive to me during the test. The closest options I found were the horizontal and vertical camera movement speed sliders under Graphics > Camera / Direction. I didn’t have enough time to determine whether they fully solve the problem. If the global client adds dedicated mouse sensitivity or FPS-cap options at release, I’ll update this section.

My advice is simple: Spend ten minutes in the Settings menu before your first serious play session. Getting your targeting, graphics, camera, HUD, and keybinds right early makes AION 2 noticeably more comfortable to play. Have a great trip to Atreia!
