Which is which is worked out from the navmesh; where a roof looks wrong, switch to Solid or Hidden in this panel. Far away the ghosts turn solid again. See inside: while you hold Alt , the rock, building and tree blocks around the cursor between you and the floor you are aiming at fade away (a cave roof, a canopy, a bridge, an upper floor, a wall), so you see the cave floor, the forest floor or the room behind. A selected marker gets the same lens when something covers it. The slice hides every rock, building and tree block above a height; its arrows ( PgUp PgDn ) step through the floors under the view centre, for stacked floors such as a city built in levels. On the Abyss maps every island is made of these blocks, so the slice cuts through islands too. Markers are small voxel models, most of them the game's own map icons. Monsters are the game's models turned into blocks. Searching never zooms in: matches are outlined in gold (the rest of their layer greys out), the view pans and backs out only if needed; Esc clears it. Tilting toward straight down turns the view into a flat map by degrees ( M tilts all the way). This is a stylised view. Ground heights are rounded to the block size, so use the cards for heights. Built from the game client's own map data: the heightmap, simplified rock and building models, tree positions, and the spawn, object, gathering and NPC tables. The maps and dungeons in Global's client take their spawns, objects and tables from the Global launch client; Eltnen, Morheim, the Rift Zone and the three dungeons Global lacks from the Asian release's (TW) client, as the tag on each card says. Tree shapes are stand-ins and the lighting is this viewer's. Maps marked "not open at launch" are closed on Global at launch. See-through R 3D Map ?
