Choose the fight you want: duels for practice, Arena or Battlefield for matches, and Spacetime Rifts or the Abyss for contested exploration. Prepare your build for that mode and give your group a clear objective.

<h2 id="optional-pvp-and-danger-zones">Is PvP optional?</h2>

Ordinary open-world player combat uses a PvE/PvP flag. In the Abyss, plan for opposing players even while completing PvE objectives.

Enemy guard NPCs can attack you even with player combat disabled. For exploration through a rift, avoid enemy settlements and plan a route around guards.

You can choose not to queue for a ranked match. For progression tasks in contested areas, weigh the reward against the risk and go with a group when you need support. The [rift guide](/spacetime-rift) explains entry, timing, and return decisions separately.

<h2 id="pvp-mode-comparison">Compare the modes before queuing</h2>

| Mode | Format | Combat focus | Useful first decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Duel | Direct challenge to another player | Reaction practice | Rehearse movement and recovery with a friend |
| Arena | Ranked matches | Target pressure and survival | Read the equipment rules and prepare the right setup |
| Battlefield | Team objective match | Teamwork and win conditions | Coordinate targets and group entry |
| Spacetime Rift | Opposing faction’s territory | Exploration and player encounters | Plan the route around hostile guards |
| Abyss | Contested PvE and faction combat | Objectives amid opposing players | Stay near support and keep a return route |
| Artifact Siege | Large group objectives in the Abyss | Coordinated control | Follow your group’s calls and event objectives |

Before joining, read the mode’s admission requirements, equipment treatment, group-entry rules, and rewards. Choose a setup for the objective rather than carrying your boss-damage loop into every match.

<h2 id="factions-and-server-pairings">Which opposing server will you fight?</h2>

Elyos and Asmodians have separate faction servers. For cross-faction encounters, an Elyos server is paired with an Asmodian server. Pairings can change periodically, so use the current pairing for your region when organizing an Abyss group.

Coordinate region, faction, and server with friends using the [server guide](/server). These choices determine which faction you join and the server pairing you face.

<h2 id="abyss-points-and-objectives">Abyss Points and practical objectives</h2>

Check the currency costs shown for your Stigma and PvP equipment upgrades, then choose missions or matches offering the rewards you need. Look for suitable objectives in Abyss missions, seasonal tasks, field bosses, Battlefield, and Artifact Siege; choose rewards that advance your next upgrade.

For a first visit, choose one manageable objective near a safe return route. Read its completion condition and reward before accepting more tasks. If it requires player defeats, do not mistake it for a peaceful exploration mission. Compare the displayed cost of your next Stigma or equipment purchase before spending resources. The [builds guide](/builds) helps connect those choices to your actual setup.

<h3>Choose a fight that teaches the right thing</h3>

| Today's goal | Useful starting example | Check before committing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Learn to react to another player | A friendly duel | Agree that this is practice and test movement and recovery rather than treating the result as class balance |
| Play an objective with friends | Inspect the currently available Battlefield or other team mode | Read group-entry rules, win conditions, availability, and actual equipment treatment |
| Finish a quest across a rift | A focused route to one displayed objective | Check whether it requires player combat and avoid assuming a PvE flag protects you from guards |

A friendly duel lets you practice target access, disengaging, and emergency recovery. For team matches, add objective calls and staying within support range to that practice.

<h2 id="first-pvp-session">A plan for your first PvP session</h2>

1. Practice a duel or another available low-commitment encounter to learn target access, defense, and recovery controls.
2. Select one mode, read its current rules, and check whether you can enter with your friends.
3. Prepare reachable defensive and support buttons; do not hide every reaction inside a PvE damage sequence.
4. In a contested zone, stay with the group and identify the route back before chasing an opponent.
5. After the attempt, identify what actually caused the loss: positioning, equipment, a missed reaction, or an unclear team objective.

Use the [class comparison](/classes) and [tier comparison](/tier-list) to choose a role, then prepare it for your team’s actual objective.

<h3>Two small exercises before chasing kills</h3>

For reaction practice, ask a friend to move while you try to keep target access. Then deliberately stop attacking to reach your defensive or recovery control. If that response is buried inside your PvE sequence, fix access before judging the class or equipment.

For a team attempt, read one current completion or win condition together. Decide who calls the objective and when the group regroups. Frontline players should avoid extending beyond reachable support; support players should check whether they can reach allies safely; damage dealers should follow the agreed target instead of leaving the objective to chase a distant opponent.

<h2 id="timers-and-membership-limits">Plan around event windows and reward limits</h2>

Check the event window, remaining Abyss time, and weekly reward counter before organizing a long session. Prioritize the objective that advances your next upgrade. The [monetization guide](/monetization) covers membership choices separately.
