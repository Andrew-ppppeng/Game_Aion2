<h2 id="what-each-list-measures">What the launch ratings measure</h2>

These launch ratings describe early solo comfort, party contribution and Abyss utility. They are not measurements of current endgame damage. Compare the role and combat loop for your intended activity before changing class.

<h2 id="spiritmaster-and-ranger">Spiritmaster and Ranger: match the playstyle</h2>

Spiritmaster keeps a summoned spirit active and reacts to short follow-up opportunities before they expire. Compare managing those spirit attacks with keeping your own ranged attacks active on Ranger.

Ranger deals physical damage at range. Keep MP sustainable and use follow-ups when their target conditions are met. Practice maintaining attacks while moving out of danger.

<h2 id="choose-for-your-content">Choose for the content you will play</h2>

| Your priority | What to compare before choosing |
| --- | --- |
| Solo quests and exploration | Recovery, safe pulls, movement, and whether you enjoy the basic rotation |
| Learning group dungeons | Tank positioning, healing, cleanses, protection, and reliable mechanics |
| Faster organized clears | Full-party benefit, damage uptime, buff overlap, and encounter execution |
| Duels or small fights | Target access, control, escape tools, and your ability to react |
| Large group PvP | Staying with your group, coordinated control, support reach, and survival |

If your friends already have damage dealers, fill a role the group needs and that you enjoy. Compare the [eight class identities](/classes), then use the relevant [starting builds](/builds). For competitive play, choose around the [PvP mode](/pvp) and your team’s needs.

<h3>Worked choice: Spiritmaster for a first solo character</h3>

For quests, exploration, and occasional dungeons, keep Spiritmaster on your shortlist if spirit management appeals to you. Try coordinating your attacks and summons through a moving encounter. A comfortable summon loop is more useful for this choice than a single burst damage number.

For faster organized clears, focus on damage uptime, party buffs, and mechanics. Practice the full encounter with your group so you can see where your contribution stops and which adjustment restores it.

<h3>Worked choice: improve a Ranger attempt</h3>

Before changing class, identify what goes wrong in your Ranger run: lost attacks while moving, an unavailable follow-up, or a failed mechanic. If maintaining range is the problem, improve positioning and defensive access before investing in another character.

**Do matching tiers mean the same role?** No. Templar handles frontline control and positioning; Cleric handles healing, cleanse, and recovery.

**How should I choose for PvP?** Compare target access, control, escape tools, support, and the objective your team wants to secure.

<h2 id="test-before-rerolling">A practical check before you reroll</h2>

First, try the class with the skill options actually unlocked on your character. Then run the same encounter more than once and note why an attempt failed: mechanics, uptime, missing healing, or an incomplete setup. Compare under similar level and equipment conditions, and record whether party buffs were present. In party encounters, include buff coverage, recovery, and the attack time they create for your allies.

Keep your main if its role and controls fit you. When new skill options become available, update the setup and practice the changed timing before judging the class again.
